Short answers

  • Formula of ethanol: CH3CH2OH (C2H5OH)
  • Smallest alkane that can form a ring: cyclopropane (C3H6)
  • Dienes: cumulated (C=C=C adjacent), conjugated (alternating C=C–C=C), isolated (separated by >1 sp3 C)
  • What is a functional group: the atom/group that determines a molecule’s characteristic reactions
  • MCQ (neopentane): 2,2-dimethylpropane; MCQ (heptane): 3-ethyl-4,4-dimethylheptane
  • 4-bromo-3-methylpent-2-ene; 4-propyl-3,5-dimethylhept-1-en-6-yne (names as given)

Order of priority of functional groups

carboxylic acid > sulfonic acid > ester > acid halide > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine > alkene > alkyne > alkane

Rules of IUPAC nomenclature

  1. Select the longest chain containing the highest-priority functional group (the parent).
  2. Change the parent alkane/ene/yne ending to the suffix of that group.
  3. Number the chain from the end nearest the highest-priority group (lowest locant).
  4. Name the remaining groups as prefixes in alphabetical order.
  5. Assign the lowest set of locants; cite stereochemistry (E/Z, R/S) where needed.
  6. With several groups, the senior one is the suffix/parent; the rest are prefixes.
