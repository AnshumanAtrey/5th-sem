Hybrid

As the name suggests, Hybrid is a combination of two different frameworks.

Hybrid is a flexible framework that blends elements of both Waterfall and Agile. This allows PMs to tailor their strategy to best suit the specific needs, using the strengths of each framework.

This framework works well for projects that require a clear plan but also need adaptability as work progresses. The idea is to use the structured planning of Waterfall to define scope, requirements, and design at the beginning of the project before shifting to the iterative approach of Agile once development starts.

Review the following graphic that illustrates the phases in the Hybrid framework. Notice how the first two phases follow Waterfall’s sequential approach, while the third phase transitions into Agile’s continuous cycle of development, testing, and improving.

Now, consider an example to understand these phases in the Hybrid framework.

Aihan is managing a project for a home appliance brand that is launching a new smart air fryer. The brand needs to ensure the product meets safety regulations, hardware specifications, and manufacturing feasibility before moving into development. So, Aihan decides to follow the Hybrid framework to manage this project.

Phase 1
Analyze requirements

Aihan starts the project with structured planning, applying Waterfall principles to establish clear goals, constraints, and compliance requirements.

She collaborates with the stakeholders, including engineers and product designers, to define key functionalities, select materials, and determine production costs.

Aihan works with the team to finalize hardware specifications, minimizing the risk of costly changes during development.

Phase 2
Design

After finalizing requirements, Aihan’s team focuses on detailed product design.

The engineers create models and technical diagrams to ensure all components align with the hardware specifications. They then build a test version and evaluate it internally for durability, heat distribution, and energy efficiency.

Before moving into production, the team shares the test version with stakeholders to gather their feedback and finalize the design.

Phase 3
Develop and release iteratively

Once the team finalizes the hardware, they transition into the Agile framework to refine the software and user experience.

The development team builds the smart features, such as app connectivity, custom cooking settings, and voice controls, in iterative cycles. They also test usability and functionality in phases, incorporating user feedback to refine the interface.

Instead of launching all features together, the home appliance brand releases an initial version to a limited number of users, gathers insights, and makes final improvements before the official launch.

Summary

The blended approach of the Hybrid framework helps the PMs and teams plan for critical and essential needs while maintaining flexibility during execution.
