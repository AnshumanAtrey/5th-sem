Core phases of a project

Projects vary based on several factors, such as industry, duration, and cost.

For example, consider the differences between developing a new software for a financial institution and organizing a marketing campaign for the launch of a skincare product. While the new software development project might require months of work and significant investment, the marketing campaign project might be completed in a few weeks with a smaller budget.

Despite these differences, each project follows a structured process: a project management lifecycle.

A project management lifecycle is a series of phases that guide a project from initiation to completion. These phases help PMs plan, execute, monitor, and finalize work by ensuring that teams stay aligned with the project goals, managing resources effectively, and mitigating risks.

For PMs, understanding the project management lifecycle is essential for maintaining control over a project’s progress and ensuring successful outcomes.

Typically, PMs and project teams complete a project in four core phases, each with distinct objectives and activities.

Introduction

A boutique clothing brand approaches a digital web design agency to design and develop a new website that reflects the brand’s unique collections. The clothing brand wants to create a visually appealing, user-friendly online store.

Phase 1
Initiate and plan a project

Pedro meets the brand’s owner and creative director to define the project’s purpose: create a modern, user-friendly online store that will attract new customers and increase sales. He creates a project charter that outlines the project’s scope, objectives, key stakeholders, and the deliverables the brand expects from the digital agency.

After obtaining approval from the owner and creative director, Pedro creates a project roadmap that specifies the timeline, budget, and the teams for the project. He also conducts a risk assessment to identify potential obstacles, such as unforeseen technical challenges during the website development.

Phase 2
Execute the project

Next, Pedro conducts the project kickoff meeting with the agency’s design, development, and quality teams, communicating the project’s scope, timelines, and each team’s roles and responsibilities.

The design team works on the website layout, determining how the product images, text, and buttons might appear on the pages. They share these with the development team that develops the website by adding functionality, such as the shopping cart feature. The quality team tests the website to ensure that the website functions smoothly, loads properly on different devices, and is free of errors.

Throughout this process, Pedro ensures that the teams have the required members and tools to complete their tasks.

Phase 3
Monitor and control the project

As the teams work on their assigned tasks, Pedro constantly monitors progress. He conducts daily meetings with the teams to track their performance and address any challenges.

For example, when the development team informs Pedro about a technical issue with the website’s payment system setup, Pedro quickly adds a senior developer to the team. While the senior developer addresses the issue, Pedro reprioritizes tasks for other development team members to avoid any delay in the website’s launch date.

Once the teams develop and test the website, Pedro shares it with the clothing brand’s owner and creative director for their review and approval.

Phase 4
Close the project

After implementing the suggestions provided by the brand’s owner and creative director, Pedro and the teams deliver the final website. The stakeholders are happy with the results and proceed with the website’s launch.

Pedro ensures that he and his teams complete and archive all documentation, including design files, project plans, and post-launch strategies. He then meets the teams to discuss what went well and what can be improved for future projects. Pedro acknowledges the teams’ effort, who worked tirelessly to meet the tight deadlines. Finally, he releases the team members from the project, so they can start working on new projects.

Summary

By organizing projects into these core phases, PMs can help their teams stay organized, minimize risks, and meet deadlines. These phases enable PMs and their teams to execute an efficient, successful project and continuously improve on future projects.
